  1. Zero-Shot Test-Time Canonicalization Using Out-of-Distribution Scoring
    Dominik Lindner, Johann Schmidt, Tom Siegl, and 2 more authors
    In Machine Learning and Knowledge Discovery in Databases. Research Track, Sep 2026
    Abs DOI

    Pretrained vision models often misclassify inputs that are rotated, scaled, or sheared, even though these affine transformations leave the object class unchanged. Robustness is usually restored either by building equivariance into the architecture or by retraining with augmentation, both of which require changing or retraining the model. Test-time canonicalization instead leaves the classifier untouched. It undoes the transformation of each input, mapping it to a canonical form near the training distribution before classification. Existing canonicalizers, however, rely on a narrow set of logit-based energy scores and bespoke search procedures, leaving the design space of scoring functions and optimizers unexplored. We reframe canonicalization as out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, which lets any OOD score serve as the energy minimized over transformations. Across benchmarks ranging from handwritten characters and sketches to natural images and 3D point clouds, we systematically evaluate around twenty OOD scores and nine search algorithms, finding that distance-based scores paired with random search and local refinement perform best overall. Because canonicalizing an already-aligned input can hurt accuracy, we add a gated mechanism that transforms an input only when its OOD score indicates this is needed, preserving most in-distribution accuracy while retaining the robustness gains on transformed inputs. Code is available at https://github.com/johschm/its.

  2. Geometrically Constrained and Token-Based Probabilistic Spatial Transformers
    Johann Schmidt, Tom Siegl, Martin Becker, and 1 more author
    In 2nd International Workshop on Biomedical Image and Signal Computing for Unbiasedness, Interpretability, and Trustworthiness at ECCV 2026, Sep 2026
    Abs HTML

    Spatial transformations such as rotation and scale obscure the morphological cues needed for accurate image classification. Careful consideration is required for reliable use in high stakes settings. A model should stay robust under such transformations, expose why a correction was applied, and signal when its input is ambiguous. While geometrically equivariant architectures provide a mathematically grounded solution, they often limit model flexibility through strict symmetry constraints and incur significant computational overhead. Spatial Transformer Networks (STNs) offer a data-driven, flexible alternative for learning pseudo-equivariances to affine transformations. However, STNs have historically been restricted to convolutional architectures and suffer from training instability. To address this, we introduce a novel STN framework. It leverages the global modeling capabilities of transformers to regress the affine transformation acting on the input. For this, we decompose affine transformations into interpretable primitives, regressed under adaptable geometric constraints, thereby preventing the training instability typically caused by degenerate transformations. By sharing weights between the localization network and the classification backbone, the framework requires minimal computational overhead. Extensive experiments on challenging insect biodiversity and medical imaging benchmarks demonstrate that our approach achieves superior predictive performance under diverse spatial transformations while maintaining high efficiency. Code is available at https://github.com/johSchm/TokenSTN.

  6. Dynamic trajectories of neonatal laboratory measurements in the first 26 weeks of life
    Seyedeh Neelufar Payrovnaziri, Sayane Shome, Jean Costello, and 22 more authors
    npj Digital Medicine, Aug 2026
    Abs DOI

    Laboratory measurements are vital for obtaining quantitative updates on patient’s health status. This study focused on analyzing 1173 distinct types of laboratory measurements collected from 34,605 infants, including ~12% born preterm (\textless37 weeks gestation) and ~88% born term admitted to the Stanford Children’s Hospitals utilizing electronic health records spanning from birth to 26 weeks of age. Our objective was to uncover temporal patterns and interdependencies among the measurements to characterize longitudinal laboratory trajectories during early infancy and identify potential biomarkers during this critical window of neonatal care. Further, we explored the underlying potential clinical factors associated with the observed trend and found a significant association between observed white blood cell counts and the Phecode category, which includes congenital heart diseases (CHD). This data-driven analysis provides insights into immune trajectories during early infancy [https://neonatesmeasures2025.streamlit.app/] highlighting the potential for applying data analytics for prognosis and early medical interventions.

  7. CytoBERT: A Foundation Model for Cytometry Data
    Syed Abdul Haseeb Qadri, Bjarne C. Hiller, Felix Blanke, and 7 more authors
    Aug 2026
    arXiv:2608.14414 [cs.LG]
    Abs DOI

    Cytometry measures the complex characteristics of single cells (e.g., counts and protein expression of immune cells) and is widely used across immunological research and clinical settings. However, cytometry data is highly heterogeneous and unstandardized due to experimental protocols and the choice of measured features. While machine learning methods hold the potential to gain deeper insights into cell biology, these challenges make them difficult to apply and transfer across studies. Recent advances in foundation models can alleviate these issues, but corresponding approaches are still scarce in this field. To address this, we provide CytoBERT, a publicly available, open-source, open-weight foundation model for single-cell cytometry data with variable marker panels. Fine-tuning CytoBERT for sample-level classification demonstrates that transfer learning across heterogeneous cytometry datasets is feasible, providing a starting point for scalable, generalizable cytometry analysis.   11. Explainable AI for impact detection in smart composites: insights into transfer learning and model robustness
    Emanuel von Cramon-Taubadel, Martin Becker, and Philipp Weißgraeber
    Smart Materials and Structures, Jul 2026
    Abs DOI

    Smart composite structures have substantial utility in high-performance applications such as aerospace and wind energy, where real-time impact detection is essential for structural health monitoring (SHM). machine learning (ML) enables the use of low-cost micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS) accelerometers for impact localization; however, existing approaches often lack robustness outside controlled laboratory conditions. In this study, we propose a full-stack framework for systematic evaluation of ML methods for impact localization on smart composites. The contributions include: (i) a customizable experimental setup for efficient data acquisition, (ii) a software framework for training and benchmarking ML and deep learning models under varying conditions, (iii) a regression-based architecture with improved accuracy, robustness, and transferability, (iv) transfer learning for efficient adaptation across structural configurations, and (v) explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) components for physical interpretation. These results demonstrate improved robustness, data efficiency, and interpretability, supporting the deployment of data-driven SHM in real-world smart composite structures.

    Briefings in Bioinformatics, May 2026
    Abs DOI

  14. Quantitative assessment of neonatal health using dried blood spot metabolite profiles and deep learning
    Alan L. Chang, Jonathan D. Reiss, Anthony Culos, and 26 more authors
    Science Translational Medicine, Jan 2026
    Abs DOI

    Neonatal prematurity leads to considerable morbidity and mortality, partly because of acquired conditions such as bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP). Standard gestational age and birthweight-based classifications of prematurity inadequately capture the variation in newborns’ health outcomes, creating an urgent need to develop risk stratification tools for vulnerable newborn infants to initiate the most appropriate care pathways as early as possible. We hypothesized that the metabolic profiles of newborn infants capture additional risk information beyond current measures. A total of 13,536 newborn screening (NBS) blood spot tests from preterm infants in California with linked clinical outcomes of prematurity were used to develop an NBS-based metabolic health index to stratify preterm infants at risk for BPD, IVH, NEC, and ROP (12,096 cases with one or more conditions and 1440 controls) through a deep learning model that provides a single index score in tandem with subgroup discovery to identify individuals with the strongest metabolite biomarker signals for adverse outcomes of prematurity. This metabolic health index captured risk signals that were distinct from gestational age and birthweight and outperformed other machine learning algorithms and clinical risk variable-based models in stratifying at-risk individuals for adverse outcomes of prematurity. The metabolic health index was externally validated in an independent retrospective cohort of 3299 very premature newborns from Ontario, Canada (2117 cases and 1182 controls), which recapitulated common metabolic risk subgroups. In summary, combining widespread metabolite screening with deep learning established a generalizable biological risk metric of prematurity.

  16. Latent Subgroups: Testing the Assumptions Behind GDRO in GEORGE
    Tom Siegl and Martin Becker
    2026
    Abs PDF

    GEORGE is a two-stage method for robustness to hidden subclasses: it clusters the feature space of a model trained by empirical risk minimization (ERM) within each class and then trains a new model with group distributionally robust optimization (GDRO), using the clusters as groups. Both stages rest on assumptions that are rarely tested in isolation. We test two of them. First, we check whether the quality of GEORGE’s clusterings predicts how much the GDRO stage improves over ERM. Across ten replications on each of MNIST, Waterbirds, ISIC and CelebA, there is not a clear trend in terms of correlation. Cluster-score gating of the GDRO stage is therefore not supported. Second, we check whether GDRO is a safe objective once a group is intrinsically hard, i.e. when its classes overlap. On a synthetic four-Gaussian problem with a linear model, GDRO finds the same mean decision boundary as ERM, but its decision boundaries vary much more from run to run once the hard groups overlap strongly. When the hard groups carry no class signal at all, GDRO’s boundaries become arbitrary. This instability is not explained by lagging group weights, by groups being split by class, or by the optimizer hyperparameters. It follows from GDRO assigning near-zero weight to the easy groups, which then no longer constrain the model.

  17. Informed, but Not Always Improved: Challenging the Benefit of Background Knowledge in GNNs
    Kutalmış Coşkun, Felix Blanke, Vanja Sophie Cangalovic, and 5 more authors
    2026
    Under review
    Abs

    Biological background knowledge (BK) graphs, such as protein-protein interactions (PPIs), are widely used to guide graph neural networks (GNNs) toward biologically informed predictions, especially in low-sample, high-dimensional settings. Yet a basic question remains largely untested: do these models actually use the provided biological graph, and what happens when that graph is incomplete, noisy, or biased? In this work, we systematically probe BK utilization through random and targeted perturbations across two cancer-related tasks: graphlevel cancer subtype classification and node-level cancer gene detection. Contrary to expectations, in cancer subtype classification, uninformed baselines outperform BK-informed GNNs and graph perturbations have negligible effect, suggesting that the provided biological networks are barely used at all. In cancer gene detection, we find stronger but highly model-dependent use of the PPI graph, ranging from topology-dominated behavior to more balanced use of graph structure and node features. We complement our real-world findings with controlled synthetic graph- and node-classification experiments to provide a positive control: when graph informativeness is ensured by construction, informed GNNs outperform uninformed baselines and degrade predictably under perturbation. Overall, our results challenge the assumption that adding biological BK automatically improves GNN performance, and show that its contribution must be explicitly tested through perturbations, a shift we argue should become standard practice when reporting BK-informed GNNs. Our code is publicly available at bckrlab.org/p/bk-buster.

  19. Evolving Markov Chains: Online Mode Discovery and Recognition from Data Streams
    Kutalmış Coşkun, Borahan Tümer, Bjarne C. Hiller, and 1 more author
    IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, 2026
    Abs DOI

    Markov chains are simple yet powerful mathematical structures to model temporally dependent processes. They generally assume stationary data, i.e., fixed transition probabilities between observations/states. However, live, real-world processes, like in the context of activity tracking, biological time series, or industrial monitoring, often switch behavior over time. Such behavior switches can be modeled as transitions between higher-level modes (e.g., running, walking, etc.). Yet all modes are usually not previously known, often exhibit vastly differing transition probabilities, and can switch unpredictably. Thus, to track behavior changes of live, real-world processes, this study proposes an online and efficient method to construct Evolving Markov chains (EMCs). EMCs adaptively track transition probabilities, automatically discover modes, and detect mode switches in an online manner. In contrast to previous work, EMCs are of arbitrary order, the proposed update scheme does not rely on tracking windows, only updates the relevant region of the probability tensor, and enjoys geometric convergence of the expected estimates. Our evaluation of synthetic data and real-world applications on human activity recognition, electric motor condition monitoring, and eye-state recognition from electroencephalography (EEG) measurements illustrates the versatility of the approach and points to the potential of EMCs to efficiently track, model, and understand live, real-world processes.

    Abs DOI

    Abs DOI

    Martin Becker, Florian Lemmerich, Philipp Singer, and 2 more authors
    Data Min. Knowl. Discov., Sep 2017
    Abs DOI

    Sequential traces of user data are frequently observed online and offline, e.g., as sequences of visited websites or as sequences of locations captured by GPS. However, understanding factors explaining the production of sequence data is a challenging task, especially since the data generation is often not homogeneous. For example, navigation behavior might change in different phases of browsing a website or movement behavior may vary between groups of users. In this work, we tackle this task and propose MixedTrails , a Bayesian approach for comparing the plausibility of hypotheses regarding the generative processes of heterogeneous sequence data. Each hypothesis is derived from existing literature, theory, or intuition and represents a belief about transition probabilities between a set of states that can vary between groups of observed transitions. Thus, this work enables a novel kind of analysis for studying sequential data in many application areas.
